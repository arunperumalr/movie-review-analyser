Sam J. Miller’s Blackfish City is set in a floating city named Qaanaaq in the Arctic Ocean.
Each of the eight arms of the city is populated by inhabitants and the markings of their standing in the socioeconomic
stratum of Qaanaaq. While order in the city is seemingly maintained by algorithms and AI,
a woman arrives riding a killer whale with a polar bear beside her. Nobody knows the reason for the orcamancer’s
arrival but they would soon find out.Blackfish City is filled with imagery that are both chilling and poetic.
Sam deals out licious chunks of futuristic tech and dystopian squalor; one can feel the methane flames and taste the
hawker’s ramen of Qaanaaq.The plot unravels with no sense of hurry, interwoven by a series of notes or recordings
called “City without a Map”, that fill missing pieces, set up future showdowns or simply expound more imagery.
Blackfish City bursts with themes, ideas and sci-fi tropes such as the wealth gap, socioeconomic struggles,
the woes of the victimised and the marginalized in a future world scarred by water wars, race wars and ruled by
algorithms and filthy rich land owners.The split second expositions of past revolutions, war torn colonies and of the
city itself, that is constantly in flux, are evocative at places while meandering at the rest.Blackfish City, like any
good sci-fi novel, left me wanting more of a city where hawkers sell ramen under tarps, the sky is always tuned to a
weird shade of grey and the streets reek of hopelessness; a future I wish would remain only in our stories.",

What happens when four writer-directors (with titles such as Aaranya Kandam, Anjathe and Soodhu Kavvum in their resumes)
decide to go all-in? Sounds Super Deluxe, doesn’t it?  Writer-Director Thiagarajan Kumararaja’s Super Deluxe bobs and
weaves genre boundaries and is a boisterous mishmash of four storylines that still manages to live and breathe! The
four writers (Mysskin, Neelan K. Sekar, Nalan Kumarasamy and Thiagarajan Kumararaja) manage to pack something of a
shotgun shell with a myriad of characters, numerous catharses and story arcs. When Super Deluxe starts firing, the
“shots” (pun intended), scattered as they may be, are each well-defined and poetic in their own right. The actors in
Super Deluxe put the written ammunition to excellent use. Vijay Sethupathi as Shilpa, a transgender woman, would leave
you reeling in awe of his nuanced performance. Fahadh Faasil, Samantha, Mysskin and Ramya Krishnan are all in excellent
form. P.S Vinod and Nirav Shah’s camerawork combined with astounding production design lends a colourful, surreal touch
to the film. Yuvan Shankar Raja’s background score is minimal, effective and a playful throwback to yesteryear melodies.
Thiagarajan Kumararaja throws in quite a few witty lines all through the film. There’s one line in particular where
Fahadh retorts to being called a casteist that is pure gold but is nothing more than a random rant in the grand scheme
of things in this film (“Dhesa-bakthi, Tamil-pattru aana Jaadhi-veriya?”). Notice even the F-word becoming a running gag
when things go topsy-turvy at the interval block.  With an almost 3-hour runtime, certain places do feel slow-paced and
laborious but the payoffs more than make up for the trouble.  Super Deluxe almost gives you whiplash as it juggles dark
humor, drama, questions on existence, lust, life, relationships, faith, feminism, acceptance and the absurd. If you are
short of breath reading that, wait till you watch the film.",
